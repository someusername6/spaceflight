Hello!

I'd like your help with putting together a solo indie developer game of space dogfight, in this directory.  I already have a fairly strong idea of what I want to build -- I prototyped it as a project before, also with Claude Code, but ended up getting lost into the weeds in some architectural and balancing challenges, to the point I thought I should start anew.  I have some fairly extensive notes below.

The game is a combination of a space dogfight game, for the main gameplay, heavily inspired by freespace 2, within a roguelike structure inspired by a campaign like FTL or Battletech.  The player is the leader of a mercenary squadron, consisting of between 3 and 8 ships throughout the game.   During each mission, the player flies the main ship, while the other ships are controlled by AI; in a future iteration of the game, multiplayer would be implemented so other human players could control other ships, through the network -- but always as a coop game.  Roguelike progression happens by acquiring credits and salvage for new ships and hiring new wingman (up to a max of 8) and new weapons; no "make the ship 10% faster" unlocks or other similar modifiers.

Gameplay happens mostly within a featureless space, with a mission-specific skybox of a nebula (and potentially a sun, with a directional light source being placed in line with the sun on the background) and stars.  A cloud of dust particles is spawned around the player to provide the sensation of movement in the space (and despawned once the player is sufficiently far away, and respawned again based on player proximity).  There are 3-8 allied ships per mission, and 3-20 enemy ships, potentially spawned in waves, or under other circumstances.  There's a variety of mission types, including "destroy all enemies", "survive X amount of time from an replenishing number of enemy waves", "destroy all enemies but do not damage this one enemy", "destroy this one specific enemy", "prevent this one stationary ship from being destroyed", "destroy this one stationary ship", "scan those cargo containers / asteroids by spending enough time within range of each", "prevent the enemy from scanning these cargo containers / asteroids", and similar variants.

Ships are small and agile, 1-person fighters.  Ships follow newtonian physics, and with space drag, for ease and intuitiveness of controls, being able to accelerate up to a max speed and to decelerate to 0, and turn in all 3 dimensions; controls are keyboard only, with W/S for pitching up and down, A/D for turning left/right, and Q/E for rolls; shift increases current speed (up to a max) and ctrl reduces it (down to zero).  A togglable option is to enable "match speed", which causes speed control (but not turning) to be automatically increases or decreased to try to maintain the current distance from the currently selected target.  There's a temporary use afterburner system, which requires holding down Z, to increase past max speed.  All keys can be rebind.  

There's a reticle targeting system, displaying the corners of a square around other targets in view; < and > keys cycle through targets, with T going to the nearest enemy target.  Friendly targets are green, enemy targets are red; currently selected target is a full brightness green / red, other targets are dark.  Targets that are not within view of the screen are instead rendered as a little arrow on the corner of the screen that represents the direction the ship would need to turn to to be able to face the target.  Reticles (and arrows pointing out of the screen) are also accompanied by the distance between the ship and the target, in meters.  Enemy and neutral targets within the screen also have a crosshair that indicates where to fire the current weapon, if it's not a guided weapon, to "lead the shots" to hit it, considering the current ship speed, the target ship speed, and the projectile speed.  This leading shot crosshair appears only if the currently selected target is within range.  Finally, there is a little circle that turns into a diamond for locking a target; when a missile weapon is selected and an enemy target is within range, it becomes lockable; a small locking reticle starts moving from the center of the screen towards the target at some speed (based on the locking speed of the missile).  If the target moves out of range or out of the screen, it "breaks the lock".  Once the circle reaches the target, the target is locked (and the missile weapon that requires a lock can now be fired, as often as the firing rate of the weapon allows, without requiring a new lock).  Changing weapons to another type of weapon also breaks the lock.

There is a cross-hair at the center of the screen; the view is always in third person, with a camera fixed behind the player's ship, sitting in the horizontal center of the screen, about 3/4ths of the way down vertically.  A small HUD element on the top right of the screen renders a second camera that follows the target, as well as display its stats (name, distance, hull, shields, heat, current ammo).  A small HUD element on the top left of the screen displays health bars for all allied ships.  A small HUD element on the bottom middle displays bars for current speed, hull points, shield points, and heat.  Missiles in flight can be selected as targets, too, as well as NPC objects (e.g. container boxes or rocks to be scanned or destroyed).  A small HUD element on the bottom left shows a 2D radar.

Firing most weapons and using the afterburner increases heat; too much heat means no more firing weapons or using the afterburner.  Heat cools down over time.  Ships have a number of primary banks and secondary banks, where primary weapons and secondary weapons can be equipped.  

Primary weapons are like guns.  Some sample archetypes:
- plasma, pulse, ion: infinite ammo weapons, cause heat when fired, different characteristics for projectile speed, firing speed, color, damage per projectile, heat per projectile.  ion may do more damage to shields, and/or prevent missile locks from that target for some time, mechanics TBD.
- autocannon, railgun: finite ammo weapon, cause less heat when fired than the energy weapons.  autocannons fire fast (65ms per shot), while railguns fire slower, but projectiles are faster and have much longer range.  The fantasy of the autocannon is "shooting a lot of small bullets fast", the fantasy of the railgun is "shooting a very fast bullet from far away, not very often, for a lot of damage".
- flak: similar to finite ammo weapon, but the projectile shatters into smaller projectiles when sufficiently close to an enemy 
- red laser, green laser, blue laser: instant effect beam weapons, renders as a continuous laser beam from the ship up to a distance or up to an enemy, damage over time, heats up as used.  in range, red < green < blue; in damage, red > green > blue.  damage decays with inverse square of distance, but caps at a maximum when sufficiently near.
- lightning gun: similar to a laser weapon, but constant damage over distance; short range, rendered as lightning bolts flickering between source and target.  damage per flicker, rather than plain damage over time.
- nuclear lance: a 1-ammo, single shot long range, very powerful, laser weapon (meant to represent a nuke being used to create a high powered laser).  similar to lasers, but much longer range and damage, and just a single pulse that fades away, rather than damage over time.

Secondary weapons are missiles, with variants for missiles that are "dumbfire" rockets and can just be fired straight, and always follow the same direction, variants that require "locking" into an enemy before being fired, and try to turn to collide with the enemy:
- seeker are basic missiles that can be locked on
- rockets are missiles that require no lock but also don't turn
- dart missiles are like seeker, but faster, with less damage, and with shorter range
- cluster missiles fire 3 at a time, in a rotating helix pattern.  no locking required, they automatically try to retarget always go to the nearest enemy (potentially changing targets if there's someone else closer or a decoy)
- swarm missiles are like dart missiles, but fire at 8 at a time
- torpedo missiles are like seeker missiles, but slower, longer range, and dealing more damage
- nuke missiles are like torpedo missiles, but cause a blast with AoE damage upon explosion, and decaying damage within said AoE.  being close to the center of a nuke explosion should one-hit destroy most ships.  nukes do not explode if the missile is destroyed, but they do explode if they run out of range and there's an enemy within blast radius.  the explosion does do friendly fire.

Archetypes will be balanced to satisfy their fantasy roles in their ideal ships, becoming better or worse at different scenarios.

Finally, there are decoys: rather than launching ahead of the ship, like other weapons, they fly back and in a random direction, at a low speed.  Missiles within range of an active decoy have a 50% chance of choosing to go after the decoy, instead.  Decoys can also destroy missiles if they are collided with by the missile, even if they are not the missile target.  Decoys automatically despawn after 10 seconds.

Missiles each have their own speed and turning radius and max range before despawning.  They all have effectively 1 hitpoint; being hit with anything destroys them.

Ships have between 1 and 5 "primary" weapon banks, and between 1 and 6 "secondary" weapon banks, each bank with its own size.  Any primary weapon can be mounted on any primary bank, but the primary weapon bank size determines how much ammo is equipped, if it's a finite ammo weapon, or how much heat the weapon generates; larger bank means being able to fire for longer.  Any secondary weapon, or decoys, can be mounted on any secondary bank, but larger bank means more ammo capacity.

Weapons can be cycled through, and they can be "linked" with weapons of the same type.  So a ship with 2x blasters and 1x plasma, seeker missiles and decoys can be cycled through in combat to fire one blaster, another blaster, both blaster at once, just the plasma, just the seekers, or just the decoys.  (A special keyboard key can also just fire decoys on a decoy-equipped ship).

Ships are also differentiated by their number of hull points, shield points, shield point regeneration per speed, max speed, turning range, max heat capacity, and heat cooling speed.  Shields stop regenerating when the ship takes damage, then after some time without taking damage start regenerating again.

Heat is meant as a balancing mechanic to ensure that ships are not consistently taking damage, 100% of the time; the ideal gameplay loop has some shooting and fighting, some flying around, in alternating sessions.  Shield regeneration should also support that, as well as AI behavior to retreat for a bit to cooldown / recharge shields.  Ideal duration of burst engagement periods should be balanced for with gameplay testing; probably at least a few continuous seconds, but there should be more time not firing than firing.

Ships other than player ships have larger hitboxes than the model itself, for ease of human targeting; human player ship hitboxes should be smaller.

The AI works through a finite state machine, including some constraints to make it non-optimal in order to enable human play -- from previous experience, turns out that making an optimal AI makes this style of game unplayable by humans.  Namely:

- At most 3 (configurable) enemy ships can target / pursue the same human-controller ship
- Human AI allies prioritize protecting human players / tanking shots / causing enemies that are attacking humans to enter evasive mode, rather than getting kills themselves or protecting each other.
- All AI ships will not aim precisely at the leading position, but will instead aim at the leading position plus a random error, which has a fixed magnitude plus a magnitude which becomes larger as the target ship moves faster.  Using afterburner or moving at higher speeds should effectively cause the enemy accuracy to become worse and worse; this should allow lighter ships to keep up with heavier, slower ones (slower to move, slower to turn, more health). The size of this ratio should be a configurable property for difficulty tuning (and become smaller as the campaign progresses over missions, or for other elite enemies).
- The AI ships cycle though various states, including trying to close distance to closest target, trying to protect a target, trying to evade when being shot / being locked at / having a missile fired at / deploying decoys, maintaining current distance while following a target to shoot at them, breaking contact and doing a large loop before reengaging.  More research to be done here to determine the actual states to be used.

The inability of the AI to shoot at the right location, and non-optimal policies for how often to shoot missiles, decoy, and evade are meant to be the main levers for balancing enemy difficulty, rather than changing the enemy's health (hull and shield characteristics) or weapon damage.  Same ships behave the same, they are just poorly piloted.  (The limit on how many enemies can focus fire on a human player and the size of enemy waves are also balancing levers, however.)

Each mission is intended to last between 1 and 3 minutes each.

The human ship player being destroyed, in single player, leads to the end of the campaign.  In multiplayer, all human ships being destroyed leads to the end of the campaign.

Between missions, the campaign is divided in "sectors", each of which is a hub of potential missions.  Missions are offered as "contracts", with a reward in credits, as well as salvage rights, in a ratio to be determined when accepting the mission (similar to a battletech mission).  Destroyed ships can be "salvaged", generating "scrap" for that ship type, the primary weapons it was equipped with, or some of the ammo for the primary or secondary weapons it was equipped with that was not used.  Collect enough scrap of a ship type to make a new ship of that type, or sell it for some credits.  Salvage includes a random component to it, and should be balanced alongside with the campaign credit rewards, and the campaign economy.  Current equipment (ships, primary weapons, unused ammo / missiles / decoys) can be sold back at a discount.  Equipment does not have to be always equipped; the player has a "hangar" to store unused equipment.  (Maybe the hangar needs to be emptied / can't be transferred between sectors, maybe it can, for simplicity).

Economy and campaign should also be able to be autonomously tested, with various attempts on how to perform ship and weapon upgrades, in order to check for balance of rewards, death spirals, and progress.  Elements to add some catch-up mechanism may be added if balance turns out to be too fragile / require it.

Potentially, there are different factions, with different ship designs / different preferences for weapons.  This should flavor what ships are generated.

Gameplay loop is to start at a hangar / station / store, acquire equipment / hire wingman to replace losses, select a contract, fly a mission, collect contract rewards / salvage, repair hull / buy more ammo / decoys / missiles / new weapons / new ships, sell back unused items, get new contract, repeat.  Contracts also provide "faction influence" which provide new contracts.  Pressure to fly to new sectors may be "ran out of contracts", or "pursued by large faction and needs to escape" like FTL.  Some overarching story may be added with multiple endings, or maybe a single story arc (like FTL fighting the rebel ship), or maybe the story is kept very thin and the focus is on the game mechanics.

For the multiplayer aspect: the host controls all decisions, and new players replace one of the wingman.  Or potentially trusted players may take control of credits and equipment as well, with everyone controlling everything; setting to be decided when the MP player is created.  If the host dies, they can come back to take control of another wingman; campaign only ends if all human players are killed on the same mission.

Game is meant to run on a web browser, using three.js for the 3d gameplay combat.  Visuals are meant to remain simple and geometric.  Ship models to either be procedurally generated ahead of time, or to be "programmer art" provided by me.  Limited number of ship models and weapons, though large, rather than procedurally generated; a blaster is always the same blaster.  Game is to be controlled fully with keyboard, though maybe we add support for mouse or joystick / HOTAS.  Specific flow of what screens are in game, and HUD, to be designed and prototyped before confirmation. Most non-gameplay interfaces, including hangout and store interface should be very visual, with icons for each ship matching a sillouette of their 3d models, and identifiable SVG icons for the weapons, and icons being draggable into and out of storage, and into slots; hovering over and clicking on icons should display relevant details on a stats window.  Some research to be done on the ideal UX design for this.

The entire game should be built using an Entity-Component-System code architecture, and be as easy to maintain and extensible as possible.  New ships and weapon types and mechanics may be added and removed as we develop it.

There should be a strong simulation aspect of the game, to allow us to tune different ship and weapon stats, as well as the campaign economy.  The simulation should use exactly the same logic as the game.

The game should be fully deterministic, in order to properly support multiplayer; the only local-only aspects are visual (such as rendering the HUD, or the dust particles around player ships for movement visuals).
